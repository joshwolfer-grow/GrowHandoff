1Setting aside tax

Nothing is withheld from a 1099 check. You owe income tax plus 15.3% self employment tax, and the bill comes once a year in April.

Move 25% to 35% of every check into a separate savings account the day it lands. Pay April out of that account.

Different bank from your checking, high yield, no fee. Your CPA sets the exact percentage. If you have to guess, guess high.

2When an S Corp makes sense

An S Corp splits your profit into a salary and distributions. The salary still pays the 15.3%. The distributions do not. That is the saving, and it grows with your profit. The fee is flat: about $600 once, then $1,000 to $1,600 a year through Grow's tax guy. Past a certain profit the saving beats the fee.

File Form 2553 by March 15. It cannot be done after the year is over. Ask your manager for the tax guy.

3How an S Corp works

It is a tax election on an LLC, not a new company. You become an employee of your own business. Set a reasonable salary with your CPA and run real payroll. A salary that looks too low is what gets audited.

  1. Form an LLC in your state
  2. Get an EIN from the IRS, free
  3. File Form 2553
  4. Set the salary with your CPA
  5. Run payroll through a payroll service
  6. Separate business bank account, nothing personal
  7. The S Corp files its own return each year

Deductions lower your taxes and also lower the income a lender counts. If a house is in the next few years, tell your CPA before you file.

4Tracking expenses

A deduction only counts if you can show it.

One business bank account and one business card. Everything business goes through them, nothing personal does.

Log mileage with an app. Photo every receipt. Fifteen minutes a month to match the card statement to the receipts. What usually counts: mileage, phone, gear, software, blitz travel, half of meals, a home office if it qualifies.

5Emergency fund

Commission is uneven. The bills are not.

Three to six months of expenses in its own savings account. Not the tax account, not checking.

If you carry a card at 18% or more, pay it off first. That is a guaranteed return and nothing else on this page is. Fund it once, leave it alone, and refill it first after you use it.

6Investing

After tax, the fund, and any high interest debt, what is left gets invested. Retirement accounts first, a Solo 401(k) or SEP IRA, your CPA picks. Then a regular brokerage account.

Broad, low cost index funds, on an automatic transfer the day after commissions land.

Fees compound against you. Set an amount you can hold through a slow quarter, raise it after a strong one. What loses reps money early: stocks off social media and leveraged products.

7Real estate

Property comes after the base above exists. It makes money five ways: cash flow, appreciation, loan paydown, tax treatment, and leverage. Two structures to know. House hacking: buy a 2 to 4 unit, live in one, rent the rest, small down payment. BRRRR: buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat.

Before buying: structure filed, tax moving automatically, no high interest debt, reserves kept separate from the down payment.

Lenders want two years of filed returns, and they read the returns, not your commission.
